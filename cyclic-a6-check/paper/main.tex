\begin{abstract}
We prove the exact cyclic Ramsey number
\[
   \Rcyc(K_4,P_6^{\mathrm{alt}})=16.
\]
The only computer-assisted step classifies the extremal colorings on 15
vertices.  A SAT instance asserting the existence of an induced blue
three-vertex path in a coloring with no red $K_4$ and no blue cyclic
$P_6^{\mathrm{alt}}$ is unsatisfiable.  Its ASCII DRAT certificate was checked
independently by \texttt{drat-trim}.  It follows that every extremal blue graph
is $K_5\mathbin{\dot\cup}K_5\mathbin{\dot\cup}K_5$.  A short deletion argument
then proves the upper bound on 16 vertices.  We provide independent encoders,
validators, hashes, and a complete proof certificate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Ba\v{s}i\'c, Damnjanovi\'c, Stevanovi\'c, and Sto\v{s}i\'c introduced and
systematically studied cyclic Ramsey numbers for ordered graphs
\cite{basic-et-al}.  Their Conjecture~4.23 predicts
\[
 \Rcyc(K_a,P_b^{\mathrm{alt}})=1+(a-1)(b-1).
\]
The case $(a,b)=(4,6)$ therefore predicts the value 16.  The lower bound is
given by the familiar coloring whose blue graph is three disjoint copies of
$K_5$.  The purpose of this note is to certify the matching upper bound.

The alternating path $P_6^{\mathrm{alt}}$ has vertices $0,1,\ldots,5$ and,
in path order, is
\[
 0,5,1,4,2,3.
\]
A cyclic copy preserves the orientation of the cyclic vertex order: cyclic
rotations are allowed, while reflections are not.  All computations below use
this convention.

\section{Reduction to a finite classification}

We regard the blue edges as a graph $B$; nonedges of $B$ are red.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:p3free}
If a graph has no induced $P_3$, then each of its connected components is a
clique.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If a connected component were not complete, choose two nonadjacent vertices
at minimum distance.  The first three vertices of a shortest path between
them induce a $P_3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Certified classification]\label{prop:classification}
Let the vertices $0,\ldots,14$ carry their natural cyclic order.  If a
red--blue coloring has neither a red $K_4$ nor a blue cyclic $\A$, then its
blue graph is
\[
 K_5\mathbin{\dot\cup}K_5\mathbin{\dot\cup}K_5.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The computer-assisted part proves that the blue graph has no induced $P_3$;
the encoding and certificate are described in Section~\ref{sec:certificate}.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:p3free}, the blue graph is a disjoint union of cliques.
There are at most three components, since one vertex from each of four
components would form a red $K_4$.  Every component has order at most five,
since a blue $K_6$ contains a cyclic $P_6^{\mathrm{alt}}$ in every cyclic
ordering.  Fifteen vertices distributed among at most three components of
order at most five must therefore form exactly three copies of $K_5$.
\end{proof}

\section{The Ramsey number}

\begin{theorem}
\[
 \Rcyc(K_4,P_6^{\mathrm{alt}})=16.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the lower bound, color the edges within each of three five-vertex parts
blue and all edges between parts red.  A blue connected subgraph has at most
five vertices, and a red clique has at most one vertex in each part.  Thus this
15-vertex coloring contains neither a blue cyclic $P_6^{\mathrm{alt}}$ nor a
red $K_4$.

For the upper bound, suppose that a valid coloring on 16 cyclically ordered
vertices exists and delete any vertex $x$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:classification}
partitions the remaining vertices into three blue cliques $A,B,C$, each of
order five, with every edge between distinct parts red.  If $x$ had a red
neighbor $a\in A$, $b\in B$, and $c\in C$, then $\{x,a,b,c\}$ would be a red
$K_4$.  Hence $x$ is blue-complete to at least one part.  That part together
with $x$ is a blue $K_6$, which contains a cyclic $P_6^{\mathrm{alt}}$.  This
contradiction proves the upper bound.
\end{proof}

\section{Encoding and certification}\label{sec:certificate}

For each unordered edge $ij$ of $K_{15}$, let $x_{ij}$ be true exactly when
$ij$ is blue.  There are 105 edge variables.  For every four-set we add the
clause
\[
 \bigvee_{ij\in E(K_4)}x_{ij},
\]
which excludes an all-red $K_4$.  For every orientation-preserving cyclic
embedding of $P_6^{\mathrm{alt}}$, we add
\[
 \bigvee_{ij\in E(P_6^{\mathrm{alt}})}\neg x_{ij},
\]
which excludes an all-blue copy.  Reflected embeddings are not added.

If a non-cluster blue graph exists, it contains an induced blue $P_3$.  Its
unique center may be moved to vertex 0 by a cyclic rotation.  Its endpoints
are an unordered pair $\{u,w\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,14\}$, giving 91 cases.
For each pair introduce a selector $s_{uw}$.  Exactly one selector is true,
and the implication clauses
\[
 s_{uw}\Longrightarrow
   (x_{0u}\wedge x_{0w}\wedge\neg x_{uw})
\]
force its induced blue $P_3$.  Requiring exactly one selector merely chooses
a witness; it does not assert that the graph has only one induced $P_3$.

The resulting DIMACS instance has 196 variables and 39,859 clauses: 35,490
clauses from the base encoding, 273 selector implications, one at-least-one
selector clause, and 4,095 pairwise at-most-one clauses.  Kissat reports the
instance UNSAT and emits an ASCII DRAT proof.  The proof was checked with an
independently built \texttt{drat-trim}; the checker reports \texttt{s VERIFIED},
with 19,912 input clauses in the core, 3,427,067 proof lemmas in the core, and
123,107,659 resolution steps.

As an encoder check, we generated the base formula independently from the
definitions and compared it with the unmodified generator from the authors'
supplementary repository \cite{ord-ram-num}.  After canonicalization, both the
clause sets and duplicate multiplicities agree.  Both encoders produce 105
variables, 35,490 raw clauses, and 16,380 distinct clauses.  We also solved the
91 selected cases individually; every case was UNSAT.  This second run is a
diagnostic cross-check, while the single DRAT certificate is the formal
computational evidence for Proposition~\ref{prop:classification}.

\section{Reproducibility}

The proof package contains the independent encoder, the combined-CNF
reconstructor, model validators, unit tests, SHA-256 hashes, solver metadata,
the DIMACS instance, the ASCII DRAT proof, and the complete checker log.  The
principal hashes are
\begin{align*}
\text{CNF: }&\quad
\texttt{f59fda6c63246901b7d43f35bcca4adcbb61efff0f09cfce9b44d5b1cdd9da03},\\
\text{DRAT: }&\quad
\texttt{38a4f3ffabca676a8e1f77d090db8fadaa92d8328ee4061bf5cc55fbfc877d78}.
\end{align*}
The verification script first audits the encodings and hashes, and can then
rerun \texttt{drat-trim} on the 817,727,304-byte proof.  Software versions and
all remaining artifact hashes are recorded in the machine-readable manifest.
The complete certificate package is archived at
\url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21934069}; source code and smaller release
artifacts are available at
\url{https://github.com/rrtrytcg/cyclic-ramsey-k4-a6}.
